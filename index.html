About Me

Building at the intersection
of data & intelligence

I'm an MS Data Science student at Northeastern University, Boston, building ML systems and then trying to prove they work.

That framing came out of something specific. While building a causal inference platform, I found my own difference-in-differences implementation had 83% confidence-interval coverage instead of the nominal 95%. Rather than quietly patch it, I extended the data generator to make the underlying assumption testable, built a placebo test, and confirmed the violation was real. The failure turned out to be more informative than the feature.

Most of my work since has followed that pattern: build the thing, then build the diagnostic that would catch it lying. It's why my projects report confidence intervals rather than point estimates, and why the limitations sections in my repositories run as long as the results.

Previously interned at IBM SkillsBuild (CSRBOX) building a plant disease detection system at 89% accuracy, and at Unified Mentor building a full stock market analysis platform with every indicator written from scratch.

Causal Inference · Experimentation
CausalLens

Covers the full lifecycle of an experiment: design it, monitor it without inflating error rates, estimate the effect afterwards. CUPED, DiD, PSM, doubly robust AIPW, Bayesian A/B, uplift modelling and always-valid sequential inference, each validated against a known injected effect.

A placebo test measured a 0.45 per period pre-trend from pre-treatment data alone, then predicted the realised DiD bias of 0.37. Validated externally on the LaLonde benchmark, recovering 1,637 USD against a 1,794 USD experimental truth from a negative 8,498 USD naive baseline.

AutoML · MLOps · Full-Stack
GlassBox ML

Upload any CSV and it inspects data quality, flags target leakage, infers the problem type, trains five model families unattended, explains the winner with SHAP, checks for drift and writes a plain-English PDF report.

It selects the winning model by expected business cost rather than raw accuracy. On 284K rows of fraud data it chose XGBoost over Logistic Regression despite comparable AUC-ROC, because Logistic Regression's 0.06 precision was ruinously expensive. A concrete case for why metric choice matters on imbalanced data.
